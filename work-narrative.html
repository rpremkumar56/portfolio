The Accidental Strategist

Subtitle: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love Spreadsheets (and the Ensuing AI Takeover).

Ever find yourself in a role and think, "Huh, how did I get here?" That was me, a bit, with Corporate Strategy at Infosys BPM. One minute I'm knee-deep in engineering diagrams (pyramids of stars, anyone? Super difficult! 😊), the next I'm wrestling with market intelligence, business planning, and trying to shape multi-billion dollar revenue decisions. It's a wild ride.

It’s less about knowing all the answers, and more about asking the right questions. And then, of course, finding ways to answer them. This often involves:

  • Staring intently at Excel sheets until they confess their secrets (they usually do, eventually).
  • Building Power BI dashboards that, on a good day, tell a compelling story with data.
  • Lately, coaxing Generative AI to help automate the mundane so we can focus on the… well, less mundane. It's like having a very smart, slightly unpredictable intern.

The goal? To help the business navigate the choppy waters of, well, business. We're talking about spotting new industry solutions (hello, $35M projected revenue from a couple of those!), optimizing costs (a cool 15% YoY savings on a $3-5M budget isn't too shabby), and generally trying to make sense of a very large, very complex sales pipeline ($3B+ is a lot of pluses).

It's like being a detective, an architect, and sometimes a professional cat-herder (40+ stakeholders, C-suite included – you get the picture). And honestly? It's fascinating. It’s about fine-tuning the giant, complex machine of a corporation, one strategic initiative at a time. No pressure.

Data, Traffic, and Why AI Still Hasn't Figured It All Out (Yet)

Or, musings from the back of a Bangalore cab.

Is it Friday already? (Okay, let's pretend I didn't say that. We all know how much we wait for this day.) So yes, I’ve been commuting to the office 2–3 times a week (Emphasis on thrice, just in case my manager is reading this).

Thanks to Bangalore traffic, I’ve started doing something mildly productive: listening to podcasts. One particularly mind-bending episode got me thinking: with all the knowledge LLMs are trained on, why aren't they consistently connecting dots across domains for those *aha!* breakthrough insights? Not just spitting back info, but genuinely *thinking* in new ways.

It's a bit like my day job, actually. Drowning in data from market intelligence reports, competitive benchmarking, financial models... the challenge isn't the *amount* of data. It's finding the signal in the noise. It's about:

  • Identifying patterns that others might miss.
  • Understanding the "why" behind the numbers, not just the "what."
  • Translating complex findings into actionable strategies (like those 10+ strategic decisions on pricing and investment influenced by analyzing that $3B pipeline).

It's not always glamorous. Sometimes it feels like you're just rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic. But when you *do* connect those dots, and it leads to a tangible result – like a new market entry or a smarter investment – it’s pretty darn satisfying. Maybe AI will get there. Maybe it needs better heuristics, or just more training on *how* to be curious. Until then, I guess it's up to us humans, stuck in traffic or otherwise, to keep asking "what if?"

From Command Lines to Boardrooms (and the No-Code Gospel)

The obligatory "MBA changed my perspective" bit, with a tech twist.

Quick question: do you check LinkedIn on weekends? Feels a bit like checking work email while you’re technically “off,” doesn’t it? (Not sure why I’m starting this like this, but let’s just agree to circle back on that thought.)

Anyway, my cousin, third-year engineering, told me he doesn’t “get” coding. Wants an MBA instead. (Look who he’s talking to.) So, naturally, I showed him how to build a working project with minimal code, mostly command-line magic. Boom. Another potential no-code prodigy born.

That’s kind of my journey in a nutshell. Electrical & Electronics Engineering gave me the "how things work" foundation. The MBA from IIM Sirmaur added the "why it matters in business" layer. And my stint as Placement Coordinator? That was a crash course in corporate relations, stakeholder management, and the fine art of herding very smart, very anxious cats (aka students during placement season).

It all comes together in the strategy world. You need to understand the tech, speak the business language, and manage the people. It's about bridging gaps. Like that time as Chair of the IEEE Student Branch, where we boosted ratings and membership – that was about understanding student needs and connecting them with resources. It's all connected, see?

So yeah, an MBA doesn't magically make you a strategist. But it does give you a different toolkit, a wider lens. And sometimes, it helps you convince your cousin that maybe, just maybe, he *can* build cool stuff without becoming a hardcore coder. You're welcome, society.

The Local LLM Revolution (and How the Birthday Mailers Were Saved)

A true story. Mostly. The drama is real, though.

2023. Typical day. Wake up, office, hellos, work. First things first: the sacred birthday mailer (priorities, right?). Quick prompts to GPT, Canva magic – done. Easy peasy.

Fast forward to a hypothetical 2025: All my go-to cloud AI tools? *Blocked*. (Okay, maybe a tad dramatic, but imagine the horror!) How would the birthday mailers survive? More importantly, how would *I* survive without my digital brainstorming buddy?

Enter Ollama. Suddenly, those powerful LLMs could run... locally! On my own machine! 🤯 Sure, a bit slower, maybe not the absolute cutting edge, but the *power*! The *control*! It was a game-changer. (And yes, the birthday mailers are safe for now.)

This little adventure in local AI got me thinking. It's not just about cool tech; it's about adaptability. It's about finding new ways to solve problems when the old ways disappear. It's the essence of being proactive and constantly learning – skills that are pretty handy in, well, any job, but especially in a field that's changing as fast as AI and strategy.

So, are you team cloud, team local, or just team "whatever gets the job done and keeps the birthday mailers flowing"? Food for thought.
